What you can do

Work through a migration over several sessions. Keep a test-watching seat on new commits. Add a coach to record lessons, or a context watcher to prompt handoffs before conversations fill up.

Try it

Start with the level 4 team. Ask its registered lead to supervise one bounded run:

tap watch on -m "CSV export PR merged; verify with gh pr view; preserve existing exports; stop after 6h"
tap watch status

The watcher checks silence and context pressure. Its default evaluator may read pane transcripts; tap help watch explains that choice. A lead still owns decisions and follow-up. When the run ends, the seat that armed it runs tap watch off.

Before a seat runs out of context, have it write its decisions, paths, open work and next step to tmp/me/handoff.md. Then that seat can run:

tap self compact --handoff tmp/me/handoff.md -- "Preserve: open work and review state"

This arms compaction. The seat ends its turn and checks the resume note before continuing; arming alone does not prove recovery finished. A Claude Code seat can add --model and --effort to resume on a different model or effort.

Long runs outlast terminals and accounts. After a reboot, tap team up --last brings back the seats that were running. After you change a sign-in or environment variable, tap session relaunch --harness claude --nudge restarts those seats in place with their conversations and tells each busy one to continue. tap teams --seats shows every team's seats with their context use and, for Claude, when the prompt cache is estimated to expire.

What’s new since level 4

The team survives context changes and evolves its own working habits. Add a role when you repeatedly do its job by hand. Describe what it watches, when to act, whom to tell and when to stop. Save proven instructions as a SKILL.md so the next seat can use them.

Make your own role skills

A team with a coach, a nights watch and a context genie feels like working with a crew, and it makes development fun.

These are examples of roles users can create, not extra skills bundled in the TAP plugin:

  • A context genie watches context pressure and asks seats to write handoffs.
  • A nights watch checks new commits and reports test failures.
  • A coach records lessons and recognizes useful work.
  • A monitor samples a declared subject and reports threshold crossings or errors.

Write a SKILL.md with the role’s inputs, actions, recipient, evidence and stop condition; include a cadence if it needs one. A TOML role description helps choose a seat. It does not create a skill or schedule it. Avoid waking a model just to check whether a peer replied.

Preset example: Luna for routine tasks

This quoted example records one operator’s strengths and staffing judgments, not defaults or benchmarks. The summary describes strengths and limits; primary, secondary and avoid lists guide the team without enforcing assignments. Check account access and revise these notes for your work. This preset excerpt keeps only the effort entry from params.config; other tuning entries are omitted.

[presets.luna]
harness = "codex"
params.model = "gpt-6-luna"
params.config = ["model_reasoning_effort=high"]
metadata.intelligence = "medium"
metadata.cost_level = "very low"
metadata.speed = "very fast"
metadata.summary = "Cheap agent that is still a lot smarter than many other models. Great for automation tasks, running scripts, and executing simple operations. Less good for heavy coding."
metadata.primary_roles = ["tasker"]
metadata.secondary_roles = ["implementer", "researcher"]
metadata.avoid_roles = ["leader", "architect", "approver", "reviewer", "debugger", "prose_writer"]

After adapting and saving it with tap:config-wizard, use tap launch luna tasker. See the complete roles and presets excerpt. Astra and Luna share a model family; they are not a cross-family review pair.
