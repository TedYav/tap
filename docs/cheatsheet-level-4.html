What you can do

Build an export feature while another pair handles its documentation. Let a lead reconcile the API between lanes. Use a reviewer from another model family to question each driver’s assumptions.

Use this level when the task has several parts that can move independently. Coordination takes time and tokens; two agents are still enough for many changes.

Try it

Ask your agent to use tap:config-wizard (native plugin) or tap-config-wizard (flat host) to configure its skills. Native plugins include the complete set; flat hosts can choose the full skill kit. Then, from your initialized project:

tap launch claude lead

Tell the lead:

Use tap-lead. Add CSV export to the reports page, with tests. Split independent work into lanes, give each writer a worktree and each driver a reviewer from another model family. Bring me the reviewed result; ask me before changing scope.

What’s new since level 3

Skills give agents instructions for coordinating the work:

  • tap-lead plans the goal, staffs the team and checks the combined result.
  • tap-drive owns a lane through implementation, review and closeout.
  • tap-conduct helps a busy lead with dispatch and review routing.

A role describes a job. A preset saves a harness, model and settings you use often. Use tap presets for saved recipes and tap team for who is seated. Launch a supported model directly with tap launch <harness> [name] --model <id> [--effort <level>]; OpenCode and Cursor accept model only. Cross-family pairs can use Fable and Astra where your accounts support them.

Write down roles and model strengths

A small [roles] table gives the team a shared vocabulary. These quoted values from one operator’s configuration describe the jobs; they do not assign them:

[roles]
driver = "Drives an implementation lane. May also implement code, or delegate its lane to an implementer. See /drive"
implementer = "Implements code from a prompt or a spec"
reviewer = "Reviews code, plans, proposals, etc, either as itself, or as a particular reviewer agent definition provided to it"
leader = "Leads a TAP team, responsible for choreographing other agents, adding and removing teammates, managing teammate context. Delegates most or all of actual implementation work to other agents. May write plans, or review important parts of plans written by other agents. Pair with a conductor to save tokens! See /lead"
conductor = "A conductor assists the leader with the variety of messages received from an active TAP team. Drivers, implementers, reviewers, etc send agents to the conductor, who will selectively relay questions to the leader as needed."

The quoted /drive and /lead shorthand refers to tap:drive and tap:lead. The full excerpt includes the operator’s [intelligences] scale.

Preset example: Astra

This is one operator’s judgment, not a default or a benchmark. The summary describes strengths; primary, secondary and avoid lists record staffing preferences. Cost, speed and intelligence are qualitative notes. Metadata briefs the agents; it does not enforce assignments or prove account access. This preset excerpt keeps only the effort entry from params.config; other tuning entries are omitted.

[presets.astra]
harness = "codex"
params.model = "gpt-6-astra"
params.config = ["model_reasoning_effort=high"]
metadata.intelligence = "s-tier"
metadata.cost_level = "high"
metadata.speed = "medium fast"
metadata.summary = "Top tier frontier model from OpenAI on high effort. Use for implementation, prototyping, writing, and partnering, when high intelligence is needed."
metadata.primary_roles = ["prototyper", "partner", "prose_writer", "implementer", "architect", "approver", "reviewer"]
metadata.secondary_roles = ["driver", "leader", "researcher"]
metadata.avoid_roles = ["tasker"]

The full roles and presets example defines the other roles named here. Ask tap:config-wizard to adapt the excerpt to your config. After saving it, tap launch astra builder starts a new seat with that recipe.
