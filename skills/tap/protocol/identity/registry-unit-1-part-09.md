**Implicit caller identity:** Caller-local verbs (`register`, `unregister`, `whoami`, communication verbs' shared actor resolver, and `team shutdown`'s self-session guard) ordinarily derive an omitted pane from process ancestry. The first observable controlling TTY maps through `tmux list-panes` to one stable `pane_id`. The inventory excludes dead (`pane_dead=1`) panes before counting matches: remain-on-exit panes can retain a TTY after OS reuse. The final query explicitly targets that pane and must report the same `pane_id` and `pane_tty`, a non-empty session, and server generation in exactly four tab-separated fields. Identity resolution consumes that record without another query. A changed binding, empty session, wrong field count, or unobservable identity field fails closed. Untargeted `tmux display-message` current-pane reads are forbidden: without `TMUX_PANE`, tmux may select an attached client's active pane; `TMUX_PANE` itself is caller-controlled. Claude Code's desktop-backed Bash tools are the narrow exception for the commands whose job is to name or rebind the current seat (`whoami`, `tap in`, `/tap:self`, and SessionStart). When process/TTY observation fails, an ordinary tool's direct `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID` may select exactly one matching active Claude row. A declared Claude background daemon has its own session id and therefore never joins by the interactive row's id: its readable job `state.json` must instead report `template: bg`, the same daemon session id as the tool environment, an exact `<acting-team>/<handle>` owner name, a cwd resolving to the acting team root, and a creation time whose latest lineage record names the row's current immutable instance; only that named active Claude row is eligible. New lineage records preserve subsecond ordering; migration may seed that incumbent only at a valid recorded `registered_at`, so older jobs refuse rather than being authorized by an invented epoch history; a legacy whole-second record in the job's creation second is ambiguous and refuses. Only `tap in` may accept a still-launching row. Either selection confers nothing until the row's recorded pane is freshly observed with the same pane id, session name, and server generation; when a server socket is recorded it must also match and pins subsequent pane operations, while a legacy row without one remains readable through the default namespace without gaining invented provenance. If the ambient/default server cannot answer for a background job, the selected row's recorded socket may be used to establish the same positive caller-outside verdict before recovery; absence of a controlling terminal or any other unobservable result still refuses. A job-selected instance and its complete pane/session/generation/socket binding are carried through and compared again at the final registry read, locked registration write, and every `/tap:self` pane-effect boundary, including interrupts, flashes, draft clearing, command/payload injection, deferred compact submission, and its later resume ping. A background job record names its interactive owner but attests neither the daemon id nor its session-store metadata as that pane's native identity, so registration preserves the row's existing identity and suppresses attribution scanning. Duplicate ids, malformed job records, stale or successor-instance rows, poll rows, and missing pane identity refuse. This fallback does not change the pure process observer or its positive `outside` verdict, so `attach` and other consumers cannot turn harness or job variables into outside/inside authority. Ambiguous or unobservable implicit identity otherwise fails closed; a consumer may treat the helper's distinct `outside` state as positive evidence only when its verb defines an outside path (as `tap attach` does above). Pass the verb's explicit identity/pane option where one exists. `team shutdown` refuses when `$TMUX` claims a tmux caller but that caller is unobservable, because its self-last ordering cannot be guaranteed; a true outside-tmux operator may still tear down the whole team with `--yes`. Registry-backed lookups still decide the handle; process ownership or the verified Claude-context row identifies only which pane row to match.

**Watcher-lease custody at the stop boundary.** `tap team down` and `tap team shutdown` disarm the watcher lease before roster removal; the reset arm (registry policy `none`) does not — reset remains the lease's owner on that path, archiving `watch/` into its checkpoint and cutting live state, so the stop engine never deletes the lease under reset.

**Index de-enrollment at the shutdown boundary.** `tap team shutdown` accepts one optional positional `<team>` naming another enrolled team; the named form rebinds the acting context to that team before effects, refuses unknown names and conflicting pinned selections, and never overrides an explicit environment pin that provably names a different bus. Once the acting registry has been cleared (observed absent, not merely requested) and no seat row is retained, shutdown also removes the stopped team's machine-index enrollment. Removal is exact and evidence-preserving: the engine captures the indexed entry verbatim — key, root, registry location, and any extra fields — into a per-run recovery artifact while holding the acting-registry sidecar lock before the index lock, refuses the write when any expectation fails (root, stored registry location, or a recreated acting registry), and deletes the artifact only after the de-enrollment succeeds. If a later self-session kill fails, the rollback restores the registry rows first and then restores the exact captured index entry from the artifact, keeping the artifact whenever that restore itself fails and naming its path for manual repair. A failed capture leaves the index untouched; an enrollment that could not be captured remains and stays purgeable through `tap admin refresh`. A retained caller row (`--keep-self` or a selection that keeps at least one seat) leaves both the acting registry file and the enrollment in place.

**Self-resolution refusal is not fallback permission:** `tap_resolve_self_handle` returns `TAP_SELF_REFUSAL=generation_unverifiable` when the caller pane is observable but the tmux server generation is not. This is distinct from an unregistered pane. **Three observed-pane states share that class, and the verb exits 2 for all three** (whoami verb closure, 2026-09-16): the generation itself unobservable; the pane/tty binding CHANGED between the inventory scan and the targeted re-query; and a targeted record that came back without a session name. All three describe a pane that WAS observed and whose identity record cannot be verified, so the remedy is `wait for tmux to answer, then retry — do NOT re-register`. The retired Bash door answered the latter two with `not_in_tmux` (exit 1), whose remedy `tap session launch <preset|harness> [name]` is false for a caller demonstrably inside tmux and whose natural next step — re-registering — is the identity hazard this class exists to block; that is a defect of the retired program, not a contract the port owed, and exit 2 has been the verdict these two cases received on the default native caller-pane path since an earlier change — stated narrowly because operator rollback routes (`TAP_RUNTIME=bash`, `$HOME/.tap/runtime-bash`, the per-verb rollback file, and the router's `--pane` operand row) did reach the Bash door, and retiring them is what this closure does. **The class is reserved for an OBSERVED pane:** a caller whose process ancestry ends with no controlling tty is `not_in_tmux` (exit 1) on every door, because telling someone outside tmux not to re-register would be the same wrong-remedy defect inverted. A malformed observed pane id on the no-arg path is a different class: `TAP_CALLING_PANE_REFUSAL=malformed pane id`, not `generation_unverifiable`, because `generation_unverifiable` would tell `whoami` not to re-register. A state-changing caller with omitted identity must refuse before changing protocol state; it may not derive a session-name handle, attribute the mutation to `unknown`, drain transport state, or continue a destructive lifecycle through a weaker identity path. Explicit identity (`<handle>`, `--by`, or `--from`) and an explicit documented force escape remain deliberate operator claims and do not depend on self-resolution. Read-only callers may fail with the same class; the overdue-inbound advisory may silently omit its warning. Callers that need both a handle and the refusal class use the in-process `tap_resolve_self_handle_into` form: command substitution would erase `TAP_SELF_REFUSAL` with its subshell.

**Handle derivation (tag-aware):** `tap register` with no name derives a **short, team-local handle** from the tmux session name. Canonical launcher session names follow `<team>/<handle>`; registration strips the team prefix and uses the handle after the slash exactly (`tap/claude` → `claude`). Legacy dash sessions remain derivable during the migration window: `<team>-<harness>[-<tag>][-<index>]` strips the team prefix and resolves a trailing `-<index>` — **`-0`/`-1` is legacy naming noise (stripped); `-N` for N≥2 is the launcher's dedup index and is KEPT** (it exists because another same-shape session does, so it is conversation identity; stripping it collided the newcomer onto the incumbent's handle — tap-bau). If the remainder is `<harness>-<tag...>` where `<harness>` is a known family (`TAP_HARNESS_FAMILIES` in `lib.sh` — the single source; currently `claude|codex|opencode|cursor|hermes|agy`), the **tag wins** and becomes the handle, with a kept index re-appended after tag resolution (`TapImprovements-claude-fable` → `fable`; `X-claude-fable-2` → `fable-2`). A bare harness stays (`TapImprovements-claude-0` → `claude`); a non-harness remainder is used as-is. When the session name yields no handle — a bare numeric session or unusable remainder — derivation falls back to the pane's **positively observed harness family** (pane-harness classify; tap-2al3d): a `claude` pane in session `0` registers as `claude` (`claude-2` on a live clash). Observed-only: the crew expected-harness override never feeds derivation, and a shell or unclassifiable pane keeps the fail-closed refusal (pass a handle explicitly).

**Duty suffixes are a CONVENTION, not a rule (tap-ypmn).** Seats are commonly named `<base>-<duty>` — `opus-reviewer`, `grok-scout`, `terra-docs-researcher`, `fable-docs` — where `<base>` is the calibration/preset name and the suffix says what the seat is doing right now. Nothing enforces it and nothing may depend on it for authority: **duty travels in the seat name and in the seat's charter, capability lives in the preset catalog and the seat-calibration ledger, and the two axes stay on two surfaces.** There is deliberately **no registry role field** — a pinned duty acquires authority by accretion, and the sanctioned retask floor keeps a handle while changing what the seat does, so a pin would lie by design. Read surfaces therefore JOIN by longest prefix (the rule `tap team` already uses for presets, below) and MUST render an unresolvable handle as seated-and-unjoined rather than absent or `gone`: a weird name fails open and visibly, while a stale pin fails closed and silently. **Any role signal — name, preset, or column — is a routing HINT, never a permission fence:** no verb may gate or refuse work on one.

**Collision resolution (never clobber, decided under the registry flock).** For the chosen handle, the incumbent is probed **identity-first with an address fallback**: its recorded `pane_id` is probed, and if that identity is dead the recorded pane **address** is probed too — a live pane at the address means the incumbent's harness restarted inside its session (**pane_recycled**, the register-side twin of the `tap team refresh` identity probe) and the seat is still occupied.
