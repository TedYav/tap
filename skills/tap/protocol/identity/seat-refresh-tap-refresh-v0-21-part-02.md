**That ownership applies to the SELF arm too, and until tap-8w0p it did not (the fix was applied to one arm and the other kept `exec`ing its engine).** `/tap:self clear`'s codes collide with refresh's own just as the peer engine's do — engine **3** *the pane is gone* against refresh's 3 *the spool scan was unstable, retry*; engine **7** *the clear was submitted and its recovery wake did NOT land* against refresh's 7 *the clear completed and the retirement was refused* — and under `--fresh` every nonzero fell into the branch that reports the seat **UNCHANGED**, which for a queued clear is false in the same expensive direction the peer arm's translation exists to prevent. Both self call sites now translate: engine **8** surfaces as refresh **9** (*native observer armed; reset remains unverified at initiating return*), engine **7** as refresh **10** (*submitted, and nothing will wake the seat — send it any message, `tap send --wake`*), engine **3** as refresh **1** (*nothing happened*, which is what 1 already means, so it spends no new vocabulary), and engine **6** — DEGRADED, the harness has no known clear command and the self clear refused rather than guessing a spelling — **also as 1**, because nothing was submitted. Engine 6 must not pass through: refresh's own 6 means *refreshed, but a sender notice failed*, a COMPLETED refresh with a partial tail, so preserving the number inverts the meaning. **The codes track next steps, not engine states**: 3 and 6 share 1 because they share a repair (nothing happened, re-running is not the fix), while 9 and 10 stay distinct because 9 is waited out and 10 is acted on. Deliberately **not 8**: refresh's 8 is the peer arm's *process replaced, rebind unconfirmed*, and a self clear replaces no process.
**The clear-completed-but-refused state is real, gets its own exit code, and is reported as such.** The clear runs before retirement and is not undoable, so this refusal is asymmetric: **context is gone, identity is not.** The seat keeps its instance, every named obligation remains settleable by that same seat, and nothing was orphaned. It exits **7**, not 1, because a destructive half that succeeded while the completion did not gets a loud code of its own, so an operator can tell it from a generic failure without reading prose. **That claim is earned here and must not be borrowed from `/tap:self clear`'s 7**, which this paragraph used to cite as its precedent: the peer path really did replace the process, externally witnessed, whereas a self clear's 7 fires before the clear executes at turn end and therefore asserts nothing about a reset (tap-8w0p). Same number, same loudness rule, different evidence. Exit **6** is the other partial state — refreshed, but a sender notice failed. Both mean "partly done"; they are separate codes because what the operator must do next differs.

**The obligation boundary.** A fresh instance begins with an empty obligation set, so anything unsettled at retirement is orphaned and its sender waits on a seat that no longer exists. `--fresh` therefore **refuses by id** (`error_class=unsettled_obligations`, exit 1) when the seat has unsettled inbound *or* its own outstanding sends — naming them, because a bare count says something is in the way without saying what, which is the shape that gets `--force` typed reflexively. The refusal is non-destructive: nothing retired, no tombstone. `--fresh --force` proceeds and notifies each waiting **sender once** (per sender, not per id — six copies of the same news is noise), stating what happened rather than instructing them to resend; they know better than the notice whether the request is still live. Cross-team senders are addressed `<team>/<handle>`, matching the canonical reply path — a bare handle would resolve in the retiring seat's own team and notify an unrelated same-named local seat, or nobody. A notice that fails to send is counted and reported with **exit 6**: the refresh did happen, so claiming otherwise would be its own lie, but those peers are still waiting and do not know, which is the outcome `--force` exists to prevent.

**Both the refusal guard and the retirement scan are instance-scoped**, the same `(handle, instance)` rule `poll`/`read`/`outstanding` apply. Counting a predecessor's payloads — which `--fresh` deliberately leaves on disk — would make every later refresh refuse until someone forced past work the canonical verbs already hide. Unstamped payloads carry no binding and are admitted, so the filter cannot swallow real mail.
