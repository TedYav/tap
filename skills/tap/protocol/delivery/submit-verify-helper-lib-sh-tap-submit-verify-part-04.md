**Watch cleanup is amortized across ticks.** The watch loop retains filename snapshots for spool sidecars, digest indexes and pane-effect tokens, passing at most 24 names per tick to the existing reapers. One kernel lock admits a cursor owner per bus. The first filename read is O(N); later batches advance the cursor. Restarting watch or replacing a directory restarts the scan. New names enter a later cycle. This bounds cleanup candidates, not the drain's delivery listing or each file's I/O time. Sender verbs gain no cleanup work or daemon. Standalone ticks and direct drains deliver messages but do no background cleanup; cleanup progress requires the long-running watch loop.

Reaping uses nonblocking metadata, obligation and reset custody. Old detached reowner leases require absent metadata, body and claim; obsolete lock-helper handshake files require the same free metadata lock. Age only selects candidates. Contention or uncertain state leaves them for another pass. Inert lock cleanup uses in-process flock. Metadata and possible-effect evidence retain their preservation rules; archive records are never garbage-collected here.

**Ownership across the hold.** A held entry keeps exactly one detached waiter, and that waiter is no longer bounded by its own deadline — it is bounded by the entry's DRAINABILITY. Before this rule every deliverer exited at the deadline, which made "redundant owners are short-lived" true by construction; holding a live entry removed that premise in both directions. A waiter that still exited would retire the only process scheduled to look again, and a waiter that merely survived would be joined by a replacement on every re-park, since native redelivery spawns one when `reownAfterRepark` returns a fresh token (measured: six owners in three seconds under a compressed lease). So a waiter intending to outlive its deadline **takes and refreshes the re-own lease**, and re-own declines to spawn through the mechanism it already uses; a waiter that dies stops refreshing, the lease goes stale, and the next re-park spawns a replacement. A waiter exits when the payload leaves the spool, and also when the entry is **escalated** — custody has moved to human disposition and every later drain skips it, so residency alone is no longer a reason to keep looping.

**The live-probe counter is serialized against its peers, and a contended write skips rather than blocks.** The count that authorizes a forced inject is read, incremented and replaced under the entry's own meta lock — the same lock the re-own lease and other live-probe updates take — so two live-probe writers cannot each read N and each write N+1. The older busy-probe and attempt writers still replace the same meta without this lock; a concurrent replacement can therefore drop a live-probe increment and delay a force, but cannot force early. The wait for the lock is BOUNDED at 0.5 seconds by default and a deadline skips without counting. At the default drain cap of 8, that adds at most 4 seconds of lock waiting against the current 20-second re-own lease; U2e owns removing that coupling. Both halves are deliberate: an unbounded wait would stall the deliverer, watch tick or scoped register recovery on a held meta lock, while a single non-blocking try would lose to brief benign contention and delay every force by an unpredictable number of drains. The two error directions are not symmetric — a skipped increment delays the force by one drain; a double increment forces early into a live pane — so the skip is the fail-closed choice. The deadline escape is untouched: a permanently contended entry still leaves at its own deadline.

**Each detached waiter drains only its entry's stored recipient pair** (`target`, `target_registry`). It validates that binding at startup and passes it to both its regular and final post-deadline drain. Same-recipient siblings remain eligible for coalescing; other recipients and global maintenance stay with their own waiters or the watch's whole-bus pass. An absent, malformed or team-mismatched binding refuses with `waiter_scope_refused`, releases the waiter's owned lease and leaves the entry parked. Missing Bun or an invalid decision also refuses; the waiter never substitutes a whole-bus drain or resolves a new binding from the handle.

**Publication's wait for the metadata lock is BOUNDED, and a timeout is a pre-effect refusal.** The pair writer (`runtime/bun/src/spool-publication.ts`) takes the entry's `*.meta.json.lock` for at most `METADATA_LOCK_TIMEOUT_MS` (5 s) and then refuses, where the retired Bash body waited forever on `LOCK_EX`. The bound is measured rather than inherited: the critical section every writer of that sidecar runs — acquire, read, mutate, atomic-replace, release — measured 0.27 ms median / 2.03 ms p99 / 41 ms worst OBSERVED over 2000 rounds of that critical section on a loaded 16-core host, so 5 s is roughly 122x that worst observed hold. The figure characterizes the benchmarked section, not a proven ceiling over every metadata writer. Both halves are deliberate, the same way the live-probe counter's 0.5 s bound is: an unbounded wait inside the send path stalls the courier on a wedged holder with no receipt and no end, while a refusal is an observable failure the sender is told about. On timeout the pair writer (`spool-publication.ts`) refuses, `tap_spool_park_placed` releases its publication claim and fails `spool_publish_failed` with exit 1, and the canonical payload is retained. The message's existing `.placed` attribution remains — both send doors place canonical before they park, and a publication refusal neither writes nor removes that receipt. Publication is reached only from certified pre-effect outcomes, so a refused park is `effect=none` **in the delivery sense that governs retry**: no `.sent`, no injection, and re-sending that id cannot duplicate delivery. It is not a claim that the pane was never touched — the Bash direct-send door cancels copy mode before it parks (gated off for `--polite`, `--no-spool` and auto-permission seats; the native door never cancels copy mode at all), so a message that later fails publication may already have had that one mutation applied to the recipient's pane. Cancelling copy mode delivers nothing, which is why it does not arm the no-retry rule. Placement is not delivery, and neither is a copy-mode cancellation.

**Removing a spool entry is one locked step, and cancel is one of its doors.** Payload, meta and the `.reown` lease are unlinked together under the entry's meta lock. Separate unlinks leave a window in which a re-own observes a payload being terminalized and mints a successor owner for an entry about to stop existing — a waiter spawned for a dead id, and a fresh lease written after the unlink. `tap cancel --spooled` therefore removes through that same locked step rather than deleting the meta itself, after archiving the withdrawn body: for a spooled entry the archived path IS the spool payload, so the archive must happen first or the removal destroys the only copy of a body the recipient never saw. Cancel keeps its own `.claim` unlink, which the shared removal does not cover.

The scope is `deadline_source = readiness-gate`, not `reason_class = busy`, and the distinction is a lifetime rather than caution. An auto-settle park (`autosettle-hold`) is a `--fyi` note waiting for a digest; its deadline is a designed hold window ending in a `.cancelled` receipt that clears the sender's queue, and holding those would extend a lifetime this rule does not own and never terminate a note nobody is waiting for.

**Possible-effect redelivery notices are unconfirmed, never failed.** After rc 5, the drain passes possible-effect evidence to the shared Bun `notice.ts` classifier. It selects `DELIVERY UNCONFIRMED`; genuine no-attempt failures retain their failure wording. At final notice selection, an existing original `.done`, verified `.sent`, or authorized `.read` suppresses the notice entirely. A read receipt must name the original ID and a reader; forced reads, retired-instance cleanup, malformed receipts, and another payload’s receipts do not count as recipient-read evidence. Digest notices exclude read members and are suppressed only when every represented member has settlement, verified delivery, or authorized-read evidence. Read-based suppression writes no `.done` or `.sent`; the ask still owes its answer. Settlement takes precedence, then observed delivery/read, then possible-effect evidence. A suppressed grace notice produces no disposition receipt or event naming an unpublished notice. Settlement after that check remains a race; no stronger guarantee is claimed. The Bash default-mode decision is removed in favor of this adapter; binding, publication, retry policy, and payload custody remain unchanged.

**No terminal-failure notice is minted while an entry is held**, and that is a correctness constraint rather than a preference: the delivery-failed notice names the canonical `payloads/<id>.md`, and that path exists only because terminalizing promotes the body immediately after. A "still queued" notice followed by a hold would hand the sender a pointer to a file that is not there. A held entry stays visible in the sender's `tap outstanding` and the addressed instance's `tap poll`. Recipient-initiated `tap read` can take an unclaimed parked body without a pane effect (§Reading your own mail).
