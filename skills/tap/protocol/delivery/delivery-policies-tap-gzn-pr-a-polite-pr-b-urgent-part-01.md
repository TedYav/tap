## Delivery policies (tap-gzn PR-A: polite; PR-B: urgent)

`tap send --delivery interrupt|now|polite` chooses delivery timing; `now` is the default. `--urgent` and `--polite` are aliases of `interrupt` and `polite`. Reply expectation and settlement stay independent of that choice.

Only explicit `--delivery interrupt` or its exact alias `--urgent` grants permission to abort the recipient's turn or tool call. Content importance, `kind=escalate`, and writer-supplied frontmatter do not. Escalations default to `now`, including an explicit `--delivery now`. The proposed P0–P3 ladder is retired; numeric levels are not accepted delivery values. Existing aliases retain their meanings and contradictory choices refuse with a usage error. `tap escalate --urgent` is a separate human-notification option and grants no pane authority.

### Delivery window (tap-7r3fn.20)

**Delivery intent is a verb, not an environment variable.** `tap send --delivery interrupt|now|polite` names it explicitly; `now` is the default. `--urgent` and `--polite` remain accepted spellings of `interrupt` and `polite`, and a contradictory pair is a usage error while an agreeing one is accepted.

**The window is a per-recipient rate limiter measured from that seat's last VERIFIED delivery**, not from when a message was enqueued. A seat that has heard nothing has no reading, and empty means due now — the first message to a quiet seat is delivered immediately. Defaults are 10 seconds for `now` and 60 for `polite`, overridable per fleet under `[delivery]` and per seat under `[delivery.overrides."<team>/<handle>"]` (a bare `"<handle>"` key is shorthand for the acting team and only answers for it). A config value is read as Python's `str()` of it and must be a run of ASCII digits — an integer, a quoted digit string (exact at any size), or a WHOLE-VALUED float (`600.0` is 600; `0.0` is 0, a legal window with no hold — the native TOML parser collapses a whole float to its integer, where the incumbent's `600.0` silently fell to the built-in); a fractional float, a boolean, a list, a table, a datetime, or a string with anything but digits degrades to the built-in, as does a config that cannot be read or parsed (a rate limit is never a reason to refuse a send). The reader is native (`runtime/bun/src/digest-window.ts`, one implementation behind the spool park, `tap digest-window` and the roster's window columns); a host with no Bun does not degrade to the built-in — it refuses with `runtime_missing`, as every native slice does, because a window nobody computed is not a window.

Send uses `deliveryDisposition` for its band policy. A bare composer injects immediately, irrespective of the delivery clock or other parked messages. Send and reply do not enumerate the spool to decide whether a ready composer may receive. A new arrival may overtake older parked mail; ordering is roughly FIFO through the drain's existing ordering, with best-effort delivery and liveness taking priority over strict FIFO (2026-09-24). Existing parked entries retain their stamped windows and custody rules. The order-shape guard still runs before placement (§The order-shape guard). Only verified delivery advances the clock; parks and unconfirmed submissions do not.

**An entry's window is stamped once, at park time**, into `hold_until_epoch`, and both the decision to park and the entry itself resolve it from one input. Window parks wait for their own epoch. A fresh draft has one grace clock, so its cooldown cannot hide its warning or postpone the clobber deadline; existing armed holds are retained. An entry with no epoch is due (it predates this change; the alternative strands it forever), and a backward clock jump makes traffic due. A recipient delivery reading AHEAD of `now` makes a new entry due immediately. For an already parked entry, `now < first_spooled_epoch` detects a backward jump and makes it due without rewriting its stored epoch. A standard `reason_class=window` park remains drainable past its synthetic waiter deadline while the recipient is present; its deliverer retains the owner lease until delivery or transfer to human disposition. Autosettle and polite deadline policies are unchanged. Digit-only short-window overrides are normalized to decimal before the direct-send decision and the spool writer consume them.

**A window park is its own reason class.** It is neither `busy` nor `safety_gate`: the pane was ready and the courier chose not to use it, so calling it `busy` would be a false statement about the recipient — and a costly one, since the busy branch mints a `readiness-gate` deadline whose expiry wakes the sender with `delivery-failed` for a seat that was never unavailable. `reason_class=window` carries `waiter_lifetime=window` and its own deadline.

**A REPLY IS ELIGIBLE `now` TRAFFIC.** `tap reply` accepts `--delivery interrupt|now|polite` and forwards it to the send it composes — it does not validate the enum itself, so one parser owns the classes and their conflict matrix. `tap reply` parks and drains exactly like any other eligible send: Reply outbounds follow the same band policy as other sends; their inbound settlement remains independent of outbound delivery. `--delivery interrupt` and `--delivery polite` remain explicit overrides on a reply. A reply parked inside the window returns exit 0 with its own queued receipt, settles its inbound immediately (settlement is orthogonal to delivery timing), writes no `.sent` until the drain verifies a submit, and is visible to the sender in `tap outstanding` throughout.

`reply=settle-only` is a **sender contract**, not a transport-enforced prohibition: the recipient may still use ordinary `tap reply` to report a problem. `tap reply --progress` is deliberately narrower and refuses that inbound class, because progress would otherwise manufacture liveness for a thread whose sender declared that no answer was owed.

### Direct attempts and delivery status

`tap send --no-spool` makes one direct attempt, bypassing both delivery windows and creating no spool or batch entry. It never presses Escape, cancels copy mode, or clears a human draft. Identity, unsupported-harness/denylist, copy-mode, pending-input, clear-fence, and recognized-modal checks still apply. A positive hold refuses visibly without parking or fallback; an ambiguous no-band observation permits the first attempt. Named readiness holds still apply: an OpenCode pane with no composer is `booting`, so `--no-spool` sends no keys, queues nothing, and tells the sender to re-send when ready. `--no-spool` conflicts with `--delivery interrupt|polite` and their `--urgent`/`--polite` aliases; it accepts the default or explicit `now` class. The message-id `send --reply-to <id>` alias refuses this send-only flag before delegating or settling its inbound; `tap reply` does not implement direct-attempt mode. Verified direct delivery advances the same recipient clock. A possible-effect result keeps the canonical payload and exits 5 without automatic re-entry.

**Batch** means the pane can take input but the recipient's window is not due. Human queued-send and spooled-outstanding rows append a read-only readiness snapshot: `[batch]` for that window wait, `[hold]` for an existing busy class other than `busy:in-turn`, and `[focus]` for the AGY-only focus wait. Ready, gone, unsafe or unreadable observations retain those distinct labels; JSON and settlement policy are unchanged. Display reads never count a probe, claim an entry, or drain the queue. **Hold** means a positive readiness or safety condition prevents input; a running turn alone does not establish a hold. These words describe timing and readiness separately. A held message is not described as one that “will not arrive”; that language is reserved for a gone recipient. Sender commands do not synchronously drain queued mail. The surviving register recovery is scoped to the successfully registered pane recipient; detached delivery and watch ticks provide the other recovery paths.

Same-ID retry (v0.23, 2026-10-12) supersedes the v0.22 no-attempt hint. The sender's text for a spool-queued or unconfirmed pane send, and its `tap outstanding` row, says **“Delivery not confirmed. Inspect with tap show <message_id>; if it is still unread, tap resend <message_id> rings it again under the same id.”**, using the actual message id. `tap poll` shows a canonical `.placed`-without-`.sent` payload to its recipient as **“unconfirmed to your pane — reading it here counts”**; it never directs the recipient to use the sender's resend verb. A spool entry alone does not earn that recipient hint. Existing JSON receipt fields and exit meanings remain unchanged.

The v0.22 successor resend (`resend_of`, fresh id, withdrawn original) is retired as of 2026-10-12. `tap resend <id>` re-rings the same message under the same id and one obligation, after any unconfirmed attempt, until the recipient observes it. An urgent or escalation original may be re-rung, but only as ordinary delivery: no interrupt key and no `urgent` or `agent-interrupt` tag, so a retry never becomes a second interrupt. The courier never automatically or silently re-enters pane delivery after a possible effect. See the [explicit same-ID retry](./reliability.md#explicit-same-id-retry).

### Policy mechanics


Machine-config edits through `tap config set` and `tap digest-window` hold the same
no-follow descriptor lock at the resolved config path plus `.lock` throughout
read/edit/write. Release closes the descriptor; the permanent lock file is never
removed or broken by age. Config set waits up to 2.5 seconds and digest-window up
to 5 seconds before refusing contention. Their existing TOML validation and
unrelated-content preservation remain the write contract.
