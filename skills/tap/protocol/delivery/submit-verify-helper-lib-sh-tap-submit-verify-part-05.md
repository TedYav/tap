**Lock-park attribution (tap-ivyyb).** When spool redelivery returns rc 4 with
`defer=lock-park`, it records `reason=lock-park` and an observation of the pane
submit lock's open holders from `lsof`, with PID, process age and executable basename from
`ps` (never command-line arguments or executable directories). Missing or incomplete observations say unavailable; an open descriptor is
an observation after refusal, not proof that its process still holds the lock.
The existing waiter class, clocks and retry behavior remain unchanged. One
courier notice per original id tells its sender that the payload is retained
and the existing delivery policy determines retry or disposition. A lock refusal
does not override a grace-window entry's conservative possible-effect evidence. It uses the existing `delivery-failed` wire kind with an
explicit `DELIVERY QUEUED` body, references `tap show <id>` rather than a possibly
absent canonical file, and never recommends a duplicate send. A deterministic
notice id and the existing one-shot notice submit prevent repeated warnings;
publication remains readable if the sender's pane cannot be woken. No lock is
broken and no lock/timeout implementation changes.

What this replaces: in one observed case a payload to a live seat mid-tool-loop was terminalized after 300 seconds and its sender was told *"the courier stopped trying ... It will not arrive on its own."* It arrived seven minutes later and was acted on. The sentence was false and it was also unsafe — it instructs the sender to re-send, and a sender who obeys manufactures a duplicate for a recipient who will read the original, which is the retry-after-possible-effect class produced by the courier's own notice. One further correction for anyone tracing a similar report: **an `.attempts` file with one row is not evidence of one attempt.** Busy probes record nothing — `noteBusyProbe` in `runtime/bun/src/spool-state.ts` increments only on `busy:pending` — so a five-minute window of retries against a busy pane leaves no trace at all.

**agy (Antigravity / Gemini, tap-luae; readiness corrected by tap-92v3r)** is a hybrid: a `>` glyph prompt like claude, but the band is a `>` row bracketed by two full-width `─` rules with a status footer below, and — like opencode — the band STAYS on screen while the agent generates. Readiness is **band presence minus a recognized permission modal**, never the footer's turn vocabulary: a `>` band between its rule pair is injectable wherever in the pane it sits, including under a `⣷ Generating…` spinner and including with a running task's card rendered BELOW it. agy queues typed input mid-turn exactly as claude and codex do (verified by hand on a live seat, 2026-09-03), so ordinary delivery types into the queue.

Readiness was footer-gated until tap-92v3r — `esc to cancel` / `Generating` ⇒ defer, `? for shortcuts` ⇒ ready — and the gate is recorded here rather than deleted, because it produced a live incident and its shape is the one a future reader is likely to re-invent. Antigravity renders a running subagent's or command's output below the input line: a third `─` rule and a task card sit between the band and the footer. A working seat with a perfectly usable band therefore read as `busy:noprompt`, the courier held the payload for its 300-second deadline, and the sender was told "It will not arrive on its own" about a seat that was one `send-keys` away. Two captured panes settle the question of what the footer was measuring: one showed a generating turn with `esc to cancel`, while the other showed an IDLE agent with a background task running and `? for shortcuts` — same geometry, opposite footers, and the difference was not whether the seat could accept input. `tap_agy_box_text` had located the band correctly in both cases the whole time. The rule-pair reader now lives once in `submit-frame.ts`; native submission uses it directly (`agyComposer`); the Bash `tap_agy_box_text` adapter is retired. This excludes footer/task-card rows from native composer text too, while preserving meaningful multiline draft whitespace. Geometry does not bypass the existing permission-modal or polite-focus gates. Native readiness now uses this same composer reader for registered `agy` recipients (tap-lwgid), rather than treating the lower rule and footer as a human draft. The shared native permission-overlay predicate runs before injection; `tap_agy_permission_overlay` delegates through `tap_bun_call`. A failed invocation or malformed result refuses, including before urgent Escape; only an exact successful `no` releases that guard.

The footer is still read, for a different question. `--polite` asks whether delivering now would break a train of thought, and readiness answers it from the last rendered line, returning `busy:in-turn` under `policy=polite` only. That split is deliberate: `busy:in-turn` is a FOCUS fact and `busy:noprompt` an INJECTION-SAFETY fact, and agy had been getting the first as a side effect of the second.

**The permission-overlay exception, and the limit of its evidence.** The retired footer gate fail-closed a permission overlay for free: an overlay that repaints the footer removes `? for shortcuts`. Band geometry alone does not inherit that, because this TUI demonstrably draws content below the band — an overlay drawn like a task card would leave the rule pair intact. The agy permission-overlay reader therefore refuses explicitly, keyed on the modal's own vocabulary (`requesting permission for:` with `do you want to proceed?` or `tab amend`), the same set this repo's seat observer already carries at `runtime/bun/src/agent-state.ts`. **The match is dialog chrome, not two phrases anywhere below the band.** Task-card rows are excluded from the scan and the confirmation must appear on the request row or within a small window below it. Both narrowings answer a defect reproduced on a real captured pane: a running task renders as `● [HH:MM:SS] <command> running`, which echoes arbitrary user text, so two ordinary shell commands (`echo "Requesting permission for: deploy"` and `grep -n "Do you want to proceed?" notes.md`) classified `busy:modal` — the one class that holds indefinitely and is never forced. A dialog is never drawn as a card row and draws its request no later than its confirm, so neither narrowing can suppress a same-row or downward-rendered overlay; a genuine overlay rendered beside a running task still holds. **The window is a provisional constant, not a measured one:** it is calibrated against the constructed fixture, and re-setting it from the real inter-phrase distance is an acceptance condition of the bead that owns the real capture. Its two failure directions are not symmetric — too narrow and a genuine overlay reads not-modal and becomes forceable, which types over a human's pending decision; too wide and this repo's own prose about the overlay holds mail forever.

**This refusal is not verified against a real capture:** every observed agy seat runs `--dangerously-skip-permissions`, so no live pane renders the modal, and its fixture is constructed and labelled as constructed. What is pinned is the refusal's polarity — a band with modal text below it defers. A real capture is the acceptance criterion of `tap-lslrc`. The band text is captured between the bottom-most `─` rule pair with the glyph/rules/footer stripped (`agyComposer`) and anchored to the LIVE bottom render — submitted messages echo into the transcript ALSO prefixed with `>`, and a repurposed pane carries a stale `agy --conversation=<uuid>` exit banner in scrollback, so a first-/any-`>` match would grab the wrong line (the render-position-anchor rule). agy classifies as an agent family and captures identity from the live `ANTIGRAVITY_CONVERSATION_ID` env var (registration/store-map lands in the tap-iee8 lane, additive). Urgent-interrupt for agy is `Escape`, sent before the payload like claude/codex (tap-92v3r; the keystroke was verified by hand on a live seat, 2026-09-03). It had no arm in `tap_interrupt_seq` until then, which was survivable only because the footer gate refused a generating agy pane outright — an urgent send to a working seat deferred, so nothing ever asked what its interrupt keystroke was. Making the band injectable removed that accident: without the arm, urgent would type into the queue while `tap send` printed "urgent — interrupting". Ordinary delivery still injects WITHOUT `Escape` and is read at the turn boundary. Normal + spooled delivery is proven live (round-trip verified against a real Gemini seat).

### Native send lock refusal (tap-7eq8)

A `lock_busy` submit result is reported as
`lock-park` before composer-state classification. Ordinary and urgent sends retain
the standard busy/readiness-gate park; `--no-spool` refuses with `lock-park` and
leaves no queued payload. This pre-effect refusal writes no attempt marker and
never claims that the recipient lacked an input prompt. Existing delivery policy
continues to determine retry or terminal disposition.
